Lombok

Just east of Bali lies Lombok, an island that feels noticeably different despite being separated by only a narrow stretch of sea. While tourism has grown rapidly in recent years, much of Lombok still feels less developed, less crowded and more connected to traditional Indonesian life. Towering above the island is Mount Rinjani, Indonesia's second-highest volcano, while white-sand beaches, surf breaks and traditional Sasak villages cover much of the coastline.

The Island Beyond the Wallace Line

One of the most fascinating facts about Lombok is that it sits on the eastern side of the famous Wallace Line, the invisible biological boundary separating Asia from Australasia.

The deep Lombok Strait between Bali and Lombok prevented many species from crossing, even during Ice Ages when sea levels were much lower. Although the islands appear almost connected on a map, they belong to remarkably different ecological worlds.

This narrow body of water is considered one of the most important biological borders on Earth and helped shape the theory of evolution developed by Alfred Russel Wallace.

The Sasak People

Around 85% of Lombok's population belongs to the Sasak ethnic group, whose language, traditions and culture differ from those found in Bali. While Bali is predominantly Hindu, Lombok is largely Muslim, creating a noticeably different atmosphere despite their close proximity.

Traditional Sasak villages can still be found throughout the island, where distinctive architecture, weaving traditions and centuries-old customs continue to play an important role in daily life.

Places We Visited

  • Kuta Lombok – The island's main tourist hub, packed with cafés, surf schools, hostels and restaurants. While it offers great access to nearby beaches, we found it much busier and more developed than expected, feeling heavily built around tourism.
  • Selong Belanak – One of the most beautiful beginner surf beaches in Indonesia, with a huge crescent-shaped bay and soft rolling waves perfect for learning.
  • Tanjung Aan – Famous for its white sand, turquoise water and stunning viewpoints overlooking the coastline.
  • Gerupuk – A traditional fishing village that has become one of Lombok's most famous surf destinations, offering breaks for all levels accessible by local fishing boats.
  • Senaru – Located on the northern slopes of Mount Rinjani, surrounded by jungle, waterfalls and home to communities that maintain distinct traditions linked to Lombok's indigenous heritage.

Our Surf Spots

Lombok quickly became one of our favorite surf destinations in Southeast Asia.

  • Selong Belanak offered long, forgiving waves that made progression easy and fun.
  • Tanjung Aan combined incredible scenery with playful surf conditions and some of the clearest water we had seen.
  • Gerupuk felt like a surf adventure, taking small boats out to reefs scattered across the bay before spending hours in the water.

The variety of surf breaks within a short distance of each other makes southern Lombok one of Indonesia's best places for surfers of every level.

Mount Rinjani: The Giant Above Lombok

Standing at 3,726 meters, Mount Rinjani dominates the island and is Indonesia's second-highest volcano.

Inside its enormous crater sits Segara Anak, a lake nearly 200 meters deep, with an active volcanic cone emerging from its waters. The entire landscape feels almost prehistoric.

Although we only visited Senaru, many travelers come specifically to complete the multi-day trek to Rinjani's summit, considered one of the most spectacular hikes in Southeast Asia.

Fun Facts & Curiosities

  • Lombok means "chili pepper" in Indonesian.
  • The island receives far fewer visitors than Bali despite being right next door.
  • The waters around Lombok are influenced by strong currents flowing between the Pacific and Indian Oceans.
  • Some beaches feature sand made from crushed coral and shells, creating unique textures and colors.
  • The nearby Gili Islands have become famous worldwide for diving, snorkeling and sea turtles.
  • Lombok experienced major earthquakes in 2018 that significantly impacted local communities and tourism infrastructure.

Our Impression

Lombok felt like a place of contrasts. Kuta was lively, busy and heavily focused on tourism, while only a short drive away we found empty beaches, fishing villages and a much slower pace of life.

The surf was fantastic, the scenery stunning, and the trip to Senaru offered a glimpse into a side of Lombok that feels very different from the beach towns. While Bali often steals the spotlight, Lombok proved to be much more than its quieter neighbor.
