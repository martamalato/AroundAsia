Sumatra

Sumatra felt like one of the wildest and most special places we visited in Indonesia. It is enormous, green, chaotic, volcanic, humid, full of jungle, full of culture, and home to things that almost do not exist anywhere else on Earth.

Unlike Bali or Lombok, Sumatra felt less polished and less designed for tourism. That made it more intense, but also more real. From orangutans in the jungle to volcanoes, giant lakes, Batak villages and some of the best food we had in Indonesia, Sumatra was unforgettable.

One of the Last Places on Earth With Wild Orangutans

One of the most unique things about Sumatra is that it is one of the only places in the world where orangutans still live in the wild. The Sumatran orangutan is critically endangered and can only be found in northern Sumatra.

Seeing them in the jungle around Bukit Lawang was one of the most amazing experiences of the whole trip. The trekking felt raw, sweaty, muddy and adventurous, but also magical: walking through dense rainforest while suddenly spotting orangutans moving calmly through the trees.

Bukit Lawang sits next to Gunung Leuser National Park, one of the richest rainforest ecosystems in Southeast Asia and one of the last places where orangutans, tigers, elephants and rhinos still technically share the same forest.

Bukit Lawang

Bukit Lawang itself was one of the most wholesome villages we visited. It sits along a river, surrounded by jungle, with small guesthouses, cafés, bridges and local families living at a very calm rhythm.

The village has a simple, friendly atmosphere. After the jungle trekking, it felt amazing to come back, eat good food, sit by the river and watch the village life around us.

It was not only the trekking that made Bukit Lawang special — the whole village had a warm and peaceful energy.

The Best Food We Had

Sumatra surprised us with some of the best food of the trip. The flavors felt stronger, spicier and deeper than in many other places in Indonesia.

  • Rendang – Originally from West Sumatra, this slow-cooked beef dish is one of Indonesia's most famous foods.
  • Nasi Padang – A full table of small dishes with rice, sambal, vegetables, meat, fish and rich sauces.
  • Mie Aceh – Spicy noodles from northern Sumatra, often served with seafood or beef.
  • Sambal – Sumatra does spicy food extremely well.
  • Fresh jungle fruit – Especially around Bukit Lawang, fruit felt simple but amazing after trekking.

Berastagi and the Volcano

After Bukit Lawang, we went to Berastagi, a cooler mountain town surrounded by volcanoes, farms and fresh air.

The area is famous for its volcanic landscape, especially Mount Sibayak and Mount Sinabung. We went up the volcano, which gave a completely different side of Sumatra: steaming vents, sulfur smells, rocky paths and views over the highlands.

Berastagi felt much cooler and calmer than the lowlands, with markets full of fruit, vegetables and flowers grown in the fertile volcanic soil.

Lake Toba: The Supervolcano That Changed the World

Lake Toba is not just a beautiful lake. It is the crater of one of the largest volcanic eruptions in Earth’s recent geological history.

Around 74,000 years ago, the Toba supereruption exploded with such force that it may have created years of global cooling. Some theories suggest it caused a dramatic human population bottleneck, possibly reducing early human populations to only a small number of survivors. Scientists still debate how extreme the impact really was, but the idea that this peaceful lake may once have almost erased humanity is insane.

Today, Lake Toba is the largest volcanic lake in the world, and in the middle of it sits Samosir Island, which is actually almost the size of Singapore.

Batak Culture

Around Lake Toba, we saw the culture of the Batak people, one of Sumatra's most distinctive ethnic groups.

The traditional Batak houses are immediately recognizable, with dramatic curved roofs that look almost like boats. Their culture has strong traditions of music, clan identity, ceremonies and storytelling.

This part of Sumatra felt very different from the jungle and the volcano areas. Lake Toba had a slower, peaceful rhythm, with villages, lake views and a strong sense of local identity.

Medan

Medan was okay, but for us it was not the highlight of Sumatra. It felt more like a big, busy transit city than a place we really connected with.

One thing we noticed was how many shopping malls and commercial centres there were. Compared to the jungle, volcanoes and Lake Toba, Medan felt much less special, although it is useful as the main entry point for northern Sumatra.

Places We Visited

  • Bukit Lawang – Jungle trekking, orangutans, river life and a wholesome village atmosphere.
  • Berastagi – Cool highlands, volcano views, markets and the climb up the volcano.
  • Lake Toba – A giant volcanic lake with Batak culture and one of the most dramatic geological stories on Earth.
  • Samosir Island – The island in the middle of Lake Toba, surrounded by calm water and Batak villages.
  • Medan – A large, busy city with many malls, useful as a transport hub but not our favorite stop.

Fun Facts & Curiosities

  • Sumatra is the sixth-largest island in the world.
  • It is one of the only places on Earth where wild orangutans still exist.
  • Sumatra is home to some of the rarest animals in the world, including the Sumatran tiger, elephant, rhino and orangutan.
  • Lake Toba was created by a supervolcano eruption around 74,000 years ago.
  • The Toba eruption may have contributed to a global volcanic winter and a severe human population bottleneck, although scientists still debate the exact impact.
  • Rendang, one of Indonesia's most famous dishes, comes from Sumatra.
  • Sumatra has dozens of ethnic groups, languages and cultural traditions.
  • The island sits on the Pacific Ring of Fire, making it highly volcanic and geologically active.

Our Impression

Sumatra felt raw, wild and deeply memorable. It was not always the easiest place to travel, but that was exactly what made it feel so rewarding.

Bukit Lawang was magical because of the orangutans and the wholesome village feeling. Berastagi gave us volcano adventure. Lake Toba gave us culture, calmness and one of the craziest geological stories on Earth. Medan was not very special for us, but it worked as the gateway to a much more fascinating island.

If Bali is beautiful and polished, Sumatra felt like real adventure.
