Ho Chi Minh City

Ho Chi Minh City, still commonly called Saigon by locals, is Vietnam's largest city and economic powerhouse. A fascinating mix of French colonial architecture, modern skyscrapers, street food culture and endless scooter traffic, it was the first stop of our journey through Southeast Asia.

History

Originally a Khmer fishing village, the city became an important trading center under French colonial rule in the 19th century. During the Vietnam War it served as the capital of South Vietnam and was known internationally as Saigon.

After the fall of Saigon on April 30, 1975, the city was renamed Ho Chi Minh City in honor of Vietnam's revolutionary leader.

Fun Facts

  • The city has more than 8 million residents.
  • There are millions of motorbikes on the streets every day.
  • Many locals still refer to the city simply as "Saigon".
  • Vietnam is one of the world's largest coffee exporters.
  • Crossing the road can feel like an adventure because of the constant flow of scooters.

Our Impression

Ho Chi Minh City was chaotic, energetic and exciting. The contrast between old colonial buildings and modern skyscrapers makes the city feel unique. The street food was incredible, the coffee culture was everywhere, and the city never seemed to sleep.
