Cat Ba

Cat Ba is the largest island in Ha Long Bay and one of the most beautiful nature escapes in northern Vietnam. It combines limestone cliffs, jungle, floating fishing villages, beaches, caves, viewpoints, and the peaceful waters of Lan Ha Bay. Compared to the more famous Ha Long Bay, Cat Ba felt more adventurous, less polished, and closer to real nature.

Cat Ba & Lan Ha Bay

Cat Ba sits beside Lan Ha Bay, a spectacular bay filled with hundreds of limestone karsts rising from turquoise water. It looks very similar to Ha Long Bay, but often feels calmer and less crowded.

Lan Ha Bay is one of the best places to kayak in Vietnam. The water is usually calm, and kayaks can pass between limestone cliffs, floating houses, hidden lagoons, and small beaches that are difficult to reach by boat.

A UNESCO Biosphere Reserve

Cat Ba is part of the Cat Ba Archipelago Biosphere Reserve, recognized by UNESCO because of its rare combination of marine ecosystems, tropical forests, limestone mountains, mangroves, coral reefs, beaches, caves, and fishing communities.

The island is important because it protects both land and sea biodiversity. It is home to Cat Ba National Park, many rare plants and animals, and the critically endangered Cat Ba langur, one of the rarest primates in the world.

Cat Ba National Park

The interior of Cat Ba is wild and green. Cat Ba National Park covers a large part of the island and protects jungle-covered limestone mountains, caves, valleys, and hiking trails.

  • The park is famous for hiking and viewpoints over the limestone landscape.
  • It protects the rare Cat Ba langur.
  • The forest feels completely different from the beach town.
  • The hikes can be hot and humid, but the views are worth it.

Caves & War History

Cat Ba is not only about beaches and boats. The island also has caves connected to Vietnam's wartime history. One of the most famous is Hospital Cave, which was used during the Vietnam War as a hidden hospital, shelter, and safe space inside the mountain.

Hospital Cave is impressive because it shows how the limestone landscape was used for survival. Instead of being just a natural cave, it became a multi-level wartime structure with rooms, corridors, and protected spaces hidden inside the rock.

Beaches & Viewpoints

Cat Ba has several small beaches near town, including the Cat Co beaches. They are not huge tropical beaches, but they are surrounded by cliffs and are easy to reach. The island is also full of viewpoints where the sea, jungle, and limestone mountains come together.

  • Cat Co Beaches: small beaches close to town, good for a swim.
  • Cannon Fort Viewpoint: one of the classic places for sunset views.
  • Lan Ha Bay: best explored by boat, kayak, or day tour.
  • National Park hikes: best for jungle and mountain views.

Floating Villages & Fishing Culture

Around Cat Ba and Lan Ha Bay, life has long been connected to the sea. Floating fishing villages, fish farms, boats, nets, and seafood restaurants show how important the bay is for local communities.

Seeing houses and fish farms floating between limestone cliffs made the landscape feel alive, not just beautiful. It was not only scenery — people actually live and work on the water.

Fun Facts & Curiosities

  • Cat Ba is the largest island in Ha Long Bay.
  • The Cat Ba Archipelago is recognized by UNESCO as a biosphere reserve.
  • Lan Ha Bay has a similar limestone landscape to Ha Long Bay but often feels less crowded.
  • The island is home to the Cat Ba langur, one of the rarest primates on Earth.
  • Hospital Cave was used as a secret wartime hospital during the Vietnam War.
  • Cat Ba has beaches, jungle, caves, cliffs, fishing villages, and kayaking all in one place.
  • Many boat tours from Cat Ba visit Lan Ha Bay instead of the busiest parts of Ha Long Bay.
  • The island has both a backpacker beach-town feeling and a wild national park interior.
  • Floating fish farms are common around the bay.
  • Sunsets over the limestone islands are one of the best parts of Cat Ba.

Our Impression

Cat Ba felt like the adventurous entrance to Ha Long Bay. It had the famous limestone scenery, but with more freedom, more nature, and a slightly rougher island feeling. The best moments were not only the views, but the feeling of being surrounded by water, cliffs, jungle, caves, and boats.

What made Cat Ba special was the mix: kayaking through Lan Ha Bay, swimming near limestone cliffs, learning about Hospital Cave, seeing floating villages, and feeling that the island still had a wild side. It was not just a beach stop — it was one of Vietnam's most complete nature destinations.
