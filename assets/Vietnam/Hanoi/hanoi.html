Hanoi

Hanoi felt like pure organised chaos: scooters everywhere, tiny plastic stools, old colonial buildings, lakes, markets, street food, coffee culture and narrow streets that somehow never stop moving.

It is one of those cities that can feel overwhelming at first, but the longer you stay, the more charming it becomes. Hanoi is loud, intense and messy, but also incredibly atmospheric and full of history.

The Old Quarter

The Old Quarter is the heart of Hanoi and one of the most fascinating parts of the city. Its narrow streets were historically organised by trade, meaning each street specialised in one product: silk, silver, herbs, bamboo, paper, shoes, metalwork or food.

Even today, some streets still keep that identity. You can walk from one street full of shoes to another full of decorations, then suddenly end up surrounded by street food stalls and coffee shops.

It feels chaotic, but also very alive — like the whole city is happening outside on the street.

Train Street

Hanoi’s Train Street is one of the most unusual places in the city: a narrow residential street where a real train passes just centimetres away from houses, cafés and balconies.

It became famous because it looks almost impossible — people drinking coffee, laundry hanging outside, tiny tables on the tracks, and then suddenly everyone moves everything away because the train is coming.

It is one of the best examples of Hanoi’s crazy mix of daily life and urban chaos.

Vietnamese Coffee Culture

Hanoi is one of the best cities in the world for coffee culture. Coffee here is not just a drink, it is a full ritual.

  • Egg coffee – A Hanoi classic made with whipped egg yolk, sugar and condensed milk.
  • Coconut coffee – Iced, sweet and almost like a dessert.
  • Salt coffee – Creamy, salty, sweet and surprisingly addictive.
  • Drip coffee – Strong Vietnamese coffee slowly filtered through a metal phin.

The cafés are often hidden in old buildings, upstairs rooms, balconies or tiny alleys, which makes finding them part of the fun.

Food We Tried

  • Phở – Vietnam’s most iconic noodle soup, perfect for breakfast.
  • Bún chả – Grilled pork with noodles, herbs and dipping sauce; one of Hanoi’s most famous dishes.
  • Bánh mì – A crispy baguette filled with meat, vegetables, herbs and sauce.
  • Nem rán – Vietnamese fried spring rolls.
  • Bánh cuốn – Steamed rice rolls filled with minced pork and mushrooms.
  • Chả cá – Turmeric fish with dill, noodles and peanuts.
  • Egg coffee – More dessert than coffee, and very Hanoi.

Lakes in the City

One of Hanoi’s nicest surprises is how many lakes are scattered around the city. They give breathing space to an otherwise intense urban environment.

  • Hoàn Kiếm Lake – The symbolic heart of Hanoi, surrounded by walking paths and old legends.
  • West Lake – The biggest lake in the city, popular for cafés, sunset walks and a calmer atmosphere.
  • Trúc Bạch Lake – Smaller and more local, close to many cafés and restaurants.
  • Hồ Bảy Mẫu – A lake inside Thống Nhất Park, a greener escape from traffic and noise.

History Everywhere

Hanoi has been an important city for more than a thousand years. It became the capital of Vietnam in 1010 under the name Thăng Long, meaning “Ascending Dragon”.

You can still feel its layered history everywhere: Chinese influence in temples, French colonial architecture, socialist monuments, war museums and modern cafés all existing next to each other.

That mix makes Hanoi feel very different from many other Southeast Asian capitals. It is not polished, but it has a deep sense of identity.

Places We Visited

  • Old Quarter – Chaotic, historic and full of street life.
  • Hoàn Kiếm Lake – The city’s classic central lake.
  • Train Street – Coffee next to the railway tracks.
  • West Lake – A calmer area for walking, cafés and sunsets.
  • Dong Xuan Market – A huge indoor market with clothes, souvenirs, food and wholesale goods.
  • Weekend Night Market – Busy, colourful and full of shopping stalls.
  • French Quarter – Wider streets, colonial buildings and a more elegant atmosphere.

Fun Facts & Curiosities

  • Hanoi is over 1,000 years old as a capital city.
  • The Old Quarter once had streets organised by craft guilds.
  • Vietnam is one of the biggest coffee producers in the world.
  • Egg coffee was invented in Hanoi when milk was scarce.
  • The city has millions of motorbikes, and crossing the road feels like a survival skill.
  • Hoàn Kiếm Lake is linked to the legend of a magical sword returned to a golden turtle.
  • French colonial influence can still be seen in Hanoi’s architecture and baguette culture.

Our Impression

Hanoi was intense, chaotic and sometimes exhausting, but also one of the most atmospheric cities we visited.

The food was amazing, the coffee culture was super fun, and the Old Quarter felt like a living museum where everything happens at once. It was not always relaxing, but it was never boring.

Hanoi is the kind of city that feels messy in the moment, but becomes more special when you look back at it.
