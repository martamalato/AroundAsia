Hoi An

Hoi An is one of Vietnam's most charming destinations, famous for its lantern-lit streets, yellow colonial buildings and rich trading history. Located on the central coast near Da Nang, the town feels like a step back in time and is widely considered one of the most beautiful places in the country.

History

Between the 15th and 19th centuries, Hoi An was one of Southeast Asia's most important trading ports. Merchants from China, Japan, India and Europe arrived here to exchange silk, ceramics, spices and other goods.

The town's international history can still be seen today through its architecture, where Vietnamese, Chinese, Japanese and European influences blend together. Thanks to its remarkably preserved old town, Hoi An was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1999.

Hoi An is also known for its long tradition of craftsmanship. For centuries, local artisans produced ceramics, lanterns, woodwork, and textiles that were traded through the port. Even today, traditional craft villages and tailor shops remain an important part of the town's identity.

Fun Facts

  • Hoi An's old town is illuminated by thousands of colorful lanterns every evening.
  • The Japanese Covered Bridge has stood in the city for more than 400 years.
  • Cars are restricted in much of the historic center, making it very walkable.
  • Hoi An is famous throughout Vietnam for its tailoring shops.
  • The town was once one of the busiest ports in Southeast Asia.
  • Thanh Ha Pottery Village is known for its traditional ceramic art.

Our Impression

Our Impression

Hoi An was one of those places that completely lived up to its reputation. The lantern-lit streets, riverside atmosphere, and beautiful architecture make it easy to see why so many people fall in love with it.

At the same time, the historic center felt more tourist-oriented than we had expected, with countless souvenir shops, tailor stores, and photo spots. We enjoyed Hoi An most when we ventured beyond the Old Town by scooter, exploring quieter neighborhoods, local cafés, rice fields, and places like Thanh Ha Pottery Village. For us, that combination of famous sights and everyday local life showed the best side of Hoi An.
