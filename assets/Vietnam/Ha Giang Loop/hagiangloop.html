Ha Giang Loop

The Ha Giang Loop was one of the most unforgettable experiences of our trip through Vietnam. For 3 days and 2 nights, we travelled through some of the most dramatic mountain scenery in the country with Motorventures: limestone peaks, deep valleys, tiny villages, winding roads and endless viewpoints that looked almost unreal.

It felt like entering a different side of Vietnam — less city chaos, more mountain silence, ethnic minority villages, rice terraces, corn fields, cliffs and roads carved into the edge of the mountains.

3 Days, 2 Nights With Motorventures

We did the Ha Giang Loop with Motorventures, which made the experience feel adventurous but still organised. The loop can be done by driving yourself or with an easy rider, but having experienced local drivers makes it much easier to relax and actually enjoy the views.

Over three days, the road constantly changed: sharp mountain bends, misty passes, tiny villages, river valleys and viewpoints where everyone had to stop because the scenery was just too beautiful to keep driving.

The best part was that it did not feel like just moving from place to place. The road itself was the main experience.

Why Ha Giang Is So Special

Ha Giang is Vietnam's northernmost province, bordering China. It is famous for its extreme karst mountain landscapes, where limestone peaks rise sharply from deep valleys.

The region is part of the Dong Van Karst Plateau Geopark, recognised by UNESCO as one of the most important geological areas in Vietnam. Some of the rocks here are hundreds of millions of years old.

Unlike flatter parts of Vietnam, Ha Giang feels vertical: mountains above you, valleys below you, and roads twisting through everything in between.

The Ma Pi Leng Pass

One of the most impressive parts of the loop is the Ma Pi Leng Pass, often considered one of the most beautiful mountain roads in Vietnam.

The road clings to the mountainside above the Nho Que River, with cliffs dropping dramatically into the valley below. It is the kind of place where photos never fully capture how huge everything feels.

This section was one of the moments where the Ha Giang Loop truly felt legendary.

The Nho Que River

The Nho Que River is famous for its bright turquoise colour as it cuts through the deep canyon below Ma Pi Leng Pass.

From above, the river looks almost unreal — a thin blue ribbon between enormous grey limestone cliffs. Many people take a boat ride through the canyon to experience the scale from below.

Ethnic Minority Cultures

Ha Giang is home to many ethnic minority groups, including the Hmong, Tay, Dao, Lo Lo and Nung. Each group has its own language, clothing, houses, traditions and markets.

One of the most interesting parts of the loop was seeing how different this region feels from the rest of Vietnam. Traditional clothes, mountain villages, corn drying outside houses and local markets made the journey feel culturally rich, not just scenic.

The mountains are not empty landscapes — they are home to communities that have adapted to living in one of Vietnam's most difficult and beautiful environments.

Places & Highlights

  • Ha Giang City – The starting point of the loop.
  • Quan Ba Heaven Gate – One of the first big viewpoints, overlooking dramatic mountain scenery.
  • Twin Mountains – Two rounded hills surrounded by rice fields and legends.
  • Yen Minh – A quieter mountain town often used as a stop on the loop.
  • Dong Van – A historic town surrounded by rocky karst mountains.
  • Ma Pi Leng Pass – The most iconic mountain road of the route.
  • Nho Que River – The turquoise river running through the canyon below.
  • Meo Vac – A mountain town known for ethnic minority markets and dramatic surroundings.

Fun Facts & Curiosities

  • Ha Giang is the northernmost province of Vietnam.
  • The Dong Van Karst Plateau contains rock formations hundreds of millions of years old.
  • Ma Pi Leng Pass is considered one of Vietnam's most spectacular roads.
  • The region borders China, and some viewpoints are very close to the frontier.
  • Many local families still live in traditional houses made from clay, wood or stone.
  • Corn is grown on steep mountain slopes because flat land is limited.
  • The loop is famous not only for landscapes, but also for ethnic minority cultures.

Our Impression

The Ha Giang Loop was intense, beautiful and completely worth it. It was not just a road trip; it felt like one of the real highlights of Vietnam.

Doing it with Motorventures made the 3 days feel smooth and fun, while still keeping the adventure. The views were huge, the roads were dramatic, and every turn seemed to reveal another mountain, valley or village.

It was the kind of experience that makes you feel very small in the best possible way.
