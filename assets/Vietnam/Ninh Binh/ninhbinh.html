Ninh Binh

Ninh Binh is one of Vietnam's most spectacular natural destinations. Towering limestone karsts rise dramatically from rice fields, rivers wind through caves, and ancient temples sit hidden among the mountains.

History

Ninh Binh played a crucial role in Vietnamese history. In the 10th century, the nearby city of Hoa Lu served as Vietnam's first capital after the country gained independence from centuries of Chinese rule.

Today, the region is recognized as a UNESCO World Heritage Site thanks to its combination of cultural significance and extraordinary karst landscape, which developed over millions of years.

Fun Facts

  • Ninh Binh's limestone mountains are over 250 million years old.
  • Many scenes from the movie "Kong: Skull Island" were filmed here.
  • Boat rowers in Trang An and Tam Coc are famous for rowing with their feet.
  • Hoa Lu was Vietnam's capital before Hanoi.
  • The area is often called the "Ha Long Bay of the rice fields".

Our Impression

Ninh Binh was one of the biggest surprises of our trip through Vietnam. The landscape almost felt unreal, with steep limestone cliffs rising directly from bright green rice fields and winding rivers.

Renting a scooter turned out to be the perfect way to explore the area. Some of our favorite moments were simply driving through the countryside, passing small villages, water buffaloes, and endless rice paddies. While places like Trang An and Mua Cave attract plenty of visitors, the region still feels peaceful and much less hectic than Vietnam's major cities.
