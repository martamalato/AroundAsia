Phong Nha

Hidden within the jungle-covered mountains of central Vietnam, Phong Nha is home to some of the largest and most spectacular caves on Earth. The region forms part of Phong Nha–Ke Bang National Park, a UNESCO World Heritage Site famous for its ancient limestone formations, underground rivers, and enormous cave systems.

History

The limestone mountains of Phong Nha began forming around 400 million years ago, making them among the oldest karst landscapes in Asia. For thousands of years, caves in the region provided shelter for prehistoric communities, with archaeological evidence showing human occupation dating back several millennia.

During the Vietnam War, many caves served as hospitals, storage areas, schools, and bomb shelters. Their vast chambers protected local communities from aerial bombardments and played an important role in daily life during the conflict.

The Giants Beneath the Jungle

  • Son Doong Cave is the largest cave in the world by volume. Some chambers are over 200 meters high and large enough to fit skyscrapers.
  • Hang En Cave, located near Son Doong, is the world's 3rd largest cave by volume and contains its own beach and underground river.
  • Paradise Cave stretches for over 31 kilometers, making it one of the longest dry caves in Asia.
  • Phong Nha Cave contains one of the world's longest underground rivers and was the first cave in the park opened to tourism.

Fun Facts

  • The national park contains more than 400 known caves, with new passages still being discovered.
  • Stalactites and stalagmites grow extremely slowly—typically only 0.1 to 3 millimeters per year.
  • A large stalagmite can take tens of thousands of years to form.
  • Some cave formations inside Phong Nha are estimated to be hundreds of thousands of years old.
  • Son Doong has its own jungle, clouds, river, and unique ecosystem inside the cave.
  • The park's limestone formations date back roughly 400 million years.
  • Several caves were used as schools, hospitals, and shelters during the Vietnam War.
  • Scientists continue discovering new species of plants and animals within the park.

Our Impression

Phong Nha felt completely different from anywhere else we visited in Vietnam. The scenery was beautiful, but it was the caves that truly amazed us. Standing next to formations that took thousands of years to grow gives a real sense of how small and temporary we are compared to nature.

Beyond the caves, we loved the relaxed atmosphere of the area. Exploring the countryside by scooter, passing rice fields and small villages, made Phong Nha feel like one of the most peaceful stops on our journey. It is a place where adventure, geology, history, and nature all come together.
