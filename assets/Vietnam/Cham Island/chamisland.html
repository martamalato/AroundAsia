Cham Island

Cham Island, or Cù Lao Chàm, is a small island group off the coast of Hoi An. It felt like a peaceful escape from the busy mainland: blue water, quiet beaches, fishing villages, palm trees, small roads, and not too many tourists. For us, it became even more special because it was the first place where we rode a scooter ourselves — and it was perfect.

A UNESCO Biosphere Reserve

Cham Island is part of the Cu Lao Cham - Hoi An Biosphere Reserve, recognized by UNESCO. This means the island is important not only because it is beautiful, but because it protects a rare mix of marine life, coral reefs, forests, beaches, fishing communities, and traditional ways of living.

The island is known for its coral reefs and marine biodiversity. Snorkeling here is not just a tourist activity — it is connected to the protection of fragile underwater ecosystems. The corals provide shelter for fish, protect the coastline, and show how healthy the sea environment is.

Size & Geography

Cham Island is not one single island, but a group of small islands. The main island is called Hon Lao, and this is where most people live and where visitors usually arrive. The island group is only around 15 kilometers from Hoi An, but it feels much further away because the pace is slower, quieter, and more local.

  • The main island has small fishing villages, beaches, forested hills, and coastal roads.
  • The water is often beautifully blue, especially around the snorkeling spots.
  • Most of the island still feels natural and undeveloped compared to mainland beach towns.
  • The island is small enough to explore by scooter, but big enough to feel like an adventure.

First Scooter Ride

Cham Island was the first place where I drove a scooter, and it could not have been a better introduction. The roads were calm, the traffic was light, and the scenery was beautiful. Instead of the chaos of Vietnamese cities, we had quiet coastal roads, sea views, small villages, and green hills.

It felt like the perfect place to learn: peaceful, simple, scenic, and not overwhelming. Riding around the island gave me a feeling of freedom — stopping whenever I wanted for a viewpoint, a beach, or just to look at the blue water.

Snorkeling & Blue Water

One of the main reasons people visit Cham Island is snorkeling. The island has coral reefs, tropical fish, and clear water, especially during the dry season. The underwater world here is part of what makes the island environmentally important.

The water around Cham Island felt much cleaner and bluer than many beaches on the mainland. It had that simple island feeling: boats on the water, rocky coastlines, small beaches, and the sense that nature was still the main attraction.

Less Touristy Than Expected

Even though Cham Island is close to Hoi An, it did not feel overcrowded. Many visitors come only for a day trip, so staying overnight made the island feel much more peaceful. After the day boats leave, the atmosphere becomes calmer and more local.

This was one of the nicest surprises: Cham Island still felt simple. There were no huge resorts everywhere, no crazy nightlife, and no overwhelming crowds — just beaches, scooters, seafood, local houses, and the sea.

Things We Loved

  • Riding a scooter for the first time in a calm and beautiful place.
  • The blue water and island views.
  • The feeling of being away from the crowds of Hoi An.
  • Small beaches and quiet coastal roads.
  • The importance of the island as a protected UNESCO biosphere area.
  • The simple local rhythm after the day tourists left.

Food & Island Life

Cham Island is strongly connected to the sea, so seafood is a big part of local life. Fishing boats, small restaurants, dried seafood, and local markets all show how much the island depends on the ocean.

  • Fresh seafood
  • Grilled fish
  • Squid
  • Simple Vietnamese rice and noodle dishes
  • Coconut drinks and cold juices near the beach

Fun Facts & Curiosities

  • Cham Island is part of a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve together with Hoi An.
  • The island group is known in Vietnamese as Cù Lao Chàm.
  • The main inhabited island is called Hon Lao.
  • It is close to Hoi An, but feels much quieter and more remote.
  • The coral reefs around the island are important for marine protection.
  • Many people visit only as a day trip, so staying overnight gives a much calmer experience.
  • The island has beaches, forested hills, fishing villages, and small temples.
  • Cham Island has worked to reduce plastic waste, including restrictions on plastic bags.
  • The best snorkeling conditions are usually during calmer sea months.
  • It is one of the easiest island escapes from Hoi An.

Our Impression

Cham Island felt peaceful, blue, and surprisingly authentic. It was not the most famous island of our trip, but that was exactly what made it special. It felt small, natural, and easy to enjoy.

For us, the memory of Cham Island is connected to freedom: our first scooter ride, quiet roads, blue sea, not many tourists, and the feeling of discovering a softer, more relaxed side of Vietnam.
