Penang

Penang is Malaysia's vibrant island state, located off the northwest coast of the Malay Peninsula. Known as the "Pearl of the Orient," it combines rich colonial history, famous street art, stunning tropical landscapes, and an internationally celebrated street food culture.

History

For centuries, Penang was mostly a quiet island covered in dense jungle before becoming the first British outpost in Southeast Asia in 1786. Established by Captain Francis Light as a free port under the East India Company, it quickly attracted diverse traders from across the globe.

Over generations, waves of Chinese, Malay, Indian, and European settlers intertwined to create a unique multicultural identity. In 2008, its historic capital, George Town, was designated a UNESCO World Heritage Site in recognition of its uniquely preserved architectural and cultural townscape.

Fun Facts

  • Penang is universally regarded as the ultimate food capital of Malaysia.
  • George Town contains the largest collection of pre-war buildings in Southeast Asia.
  • The Penang Hill Funicular Railway is one of the steepest tunnel tracks in the world.
  • The island is connected to the Malaysian mainland by two of the longest bridges in Southeast Asia.
  • Penang is a melting pot of cultures, famous for its unique Peranakan (Baba-Nyonya) heritage.

Our Impression

Penang felt like an incredible sensory experience where past and present blend seamlessly together. The combination of historic shophouses, trendy cafés, lush jungle hills, and world-class street food made it impossible to leave without a full stomach. Hunting for interactive wall murals in George Town and watching the sunset from the Clan Jetties were some of the ultimate highlights of our stay.
