Dulan

Dulan is a small laid-back village on Taiwan's east coast that has become popular among surfers, artists, backpackers, and travelers looking to slow down. Unlike Taiwan's busy cities, life here revolves around the ocean, good food, and a relaxed community atmosphere.

History

Dulan is located in Taitung County and has long been home to the indigenous Amis people, Taiwan's largest indigenous ethnic group. In recent decades, the village has attracted artists, musicians, and surfers who have helped create its unique creative and bohemian vibe.

Today, Dulan remains one of Taiwan's best-known surf towns while still preserving its small-village character.

Fun Facts

  • Dulan is one of Taiwan's most famous surfing destinations.
  • The long coastline means surfers can often find uncrowded waves.
  • The Pacific Ocean lies directly east of Dulan, with no land between Taiwan and North America.
  • The old Dulan Sugar Factory has been transformed into a cultural and artistic gathering space.
  • The village is home to a surprisingly large international community of surfers and artists.
  • Dulan's relaxed atmosphere is very different from cities such as Taipei or Kaohsiung.

Our Impression

Dulan felt like one long road stretched between the mountains and the ocean. Most of the village consists of a handful of restaurants, cafés, surf shops, and supermarkets, giving it a simple and easygoing atmosphere.

We stayed at Tranquilo Hostel and quickly understood why so many travelers end up extending their stay. The beach was perfect for surfing thanks to its long coastline and relatively uncrowded waves. One of our favorite memories was spending an evening at the old Sugar Factory, where locals, travelers, and surfers gathered for music, drinks, and a great atmosphere. Dulan may be small, but it has a unique charm that makes it difficult to leave.
