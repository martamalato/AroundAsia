Taroko National Park

Taroko National Park is one of Taiwan's most breathtaking natural wonders. Towering marble cliffs rise hundreds of meters above a turquoise river, creating a dramatic gorge that feels almost unreal. Located on Taiwan's rugged east coast, Taroko is where geology, engineering, history and raw natural power come together in spectacular fashion.

How Was Taroko Gorge Created?

The story begins millions of years ago beneath the ocean. Thick layers of limestone accumulated on the sea floor and were later transformed into marble through intense heat and pressure deep within the Earth.

Around 5 to 6 million years ago, the Philippine Sea Plate began colliding with the Eurasian Plate. This collision continues today and is responsible for lifting Taiwan out of the sea. In fact, Taiwan is still rising by several millimeters every year.

As the mountains rose, the Liwu River relentlessly carved through the marble, cutting deeper and deeper over millions of years. The result is Taroko Gorge: one of the few marble canyons in the world and one of the most spectacular examples of active mountain-building anywhere on Earth.

Japanese Influence & Building the Road

During the Japanese colonial period (1895–1945), the Japanese government built the first routes through the mountains. The construction was extremely challenging due to the steep cliffs, dense forests and resistance from indigenous communities living in the area.

Many sections were carved directly into the mountainside by hand. The famous Central Cross-Island Highway that visitors use today was completed decades later, connecting Taiwan's east and west coasts through some of the country's most dramatic terrain.

The Marble Kingdom

  • The cliffs of Taroko are composed largely of marble, not ordinary limestone.
  • The marble visible today originally formed from ancient marine sediments deposited hundreds of millions of years ago.
  • Taiwan has a significant marble industry and has exported marble products around the world.
  • Some marble walls inside Taroko rise more than 300 meters almost vertically above the river.
  • The gorge is one of the largest concentrations of exposed marble anywhere on Earth.

Landslides, Earthquakes & Nature's Power

Taroko is beautiful, but it is also a reminder that Taiwan sits on one of the most active geological zones in the world. Earthquakes, typhoons and heavy rainfall regularly trigger landslides and rockfalls throughout the park.

In April 2024, a powerful earthquake caused extensive damage across Taroko, destroying trails, roads, bridges and visitor facilities. Even today, some areas remain partially closed while repairs and safety assessments continue.

The same forces that created the gorge are still actively reshaping it, making Taroko a landscape that is literally changing before our eyes.

Fun Facts

  • Taroko Gorge took millions of years to form and is still evolving today.
  • Taiwan experiences thousands of earthquakes every year, although most are too small to be felt.
  • The mountains around Taroko rise from sea level to more than 3,000 meters in a relatively short distance.
  • The Liwu River continues to carve deeper into the marble every year.
  • Taroko is one of the few places in the world where a major gorge has been carved through marble rather than limestone.
  • The name "Taroko" comes from the indigenous Truku people who have lived in the region for generations.
  • Some sections of road were blasted directly into vertical cliff faces.
  • The park covers nearly 1,000 square kilometers of mountains, forests, rivers and cliffs.
  • Several peaks within the national park exceed 3,500 meters in elevation.

Our Impression

Taroko completely exceeded our expectations. Photos simply do not prepare you for the scale of the cliffs or the color of the marble walls surrounding the river. Everywhere we looked, the landscape felt bigger and more dramatic than we imagined.

What fascinated us most was understanding that this incredible scenery exists because Taiwan sits at the collision point of two tectonic plates. Standing in the gorge, it felt like we were witnessing geology in action. Even with some areas still closed due to recent landslides and earthquakes, Taroko remains one of the most impressive natural places we have ever visited.
