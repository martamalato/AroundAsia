Tainan

Tainan is often called Taiwan's oldest city and cultural capital. Before Taipei became the political center, Tainan was the heart of Taiwan's history, trade, religion and food culture. Today it feels slower, warmer and more traditional than the big northern cities, with temples, old streets, night markets and amazing snacks everywhere.

History

Tainan was one of the first major settlements in Taiwan. In the 17th century, the Dutch East India Company built Fort Zeelandia in Anping, using the area as an important trading base between China, Japan and Southeast Asia.

Later, the Ming loyalist leader Koxinga defeated the Dutch in 1662 and made Tainan the political center of Taiwan. For more than 200 years, Tainan remained Taiwan's most important city before the capital gradually shifted north.

Because of this long history, Tainan has some of Taiwan's oldest temples, forts, streets and traditional houses. Walking through the city often feels like discovering layers of Dutch, Chinese, Japanese and Taiwanese history all mixed together.

Food Capital of Taiwan

Tainan is famous across Taiwan for its food. Many Taiwanese people consider it the island's true food capital, especially for traditional snacks and recipes passed down through generations.

  • Tainan food is known for being slightly sweeter than food in northern Taiwan.
  • Many dishes are small, cheap and perfect for trying several things in one evening.
  • Some food stalls have been run by the same families for generations.
  • Beef soup is especially famous because it is often made with very fresh local beef.
  • Milkfish is one of Tainan's signature ingredients.

Temples & Traditions

Tainan has one of the highest concentrations of temples in Taiwan. Religion is deeply woven into daily life, with incense, festivals, processions and colorful temple decorations appearing throughout the city.

  • The city is home to hundreds of temples.
  • Many temples are dedicated to Mazu, the sea goddess worshipped by fishermen and coastal communities.
  • Tainan's Confucius Temple is often considered Taiwan's first Confucian temple.
  • Temple festivals can include firecrackers, music, costumes, dances and large street processions.
  • Small neighborhood temples often act as community centers.

Fun Facts

  • Tainan was Taiwan's capital for more than 200 years.
  • It is often called the birthplace of Taiwan.
  • Anping was once the center of Dutch colonial power in Taiwan.
  • The Anping Tree House is an old warehouse slowly taken over by banyan tree roots.
  • Shennong Street is one of the most atmospheric historic streets in the city.
  • Tainan has more historic monuments than any other city in Taiwan.
  • The city is famous for slow travel, temple hopping and food hunting.
  • Compared with Taipei, Tainan feels more traditional, relaxed and local.

Our Impression

Tainan felt like the historical soul of Taiwan. It was less polished and modern than Taipei, but that was exactly what made it special. The city had a warmer, slower rhythm, with temples hidden on busy streets, old houses, food stalls and little cafés around almost every corner.

What we enjoyed most was simply wandering around, trying different snacks and discovering old streets without rushing. Tainan felt very local and full of character, especially in areas like Anping and Shennong Street. It is the kind of city that rewards curiosity, appetite and slow exploring.
