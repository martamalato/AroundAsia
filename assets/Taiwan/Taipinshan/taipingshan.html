Taipingshan

Hidden in the mountains of Yilan County, Taipingshan is one of Taiwan's most enchanting forest regions. Once a major logging area, it has been transformed into a protected recreation forest filled with misty mountains, ancient trees, moss-covered trails, mountain lakes and historic railways. Visiting felt like stepping into a fantasy movie.

History

During the Japanese colonial period, Taipingshan became one of Taiwan's most important logging centers. Vast forests of Taiwanese cypress and cedar supplied valuable timber that was transported down the mountains using an extensive network of railways.

Logging continued for decades before conservation efforts gradually took priority. Today, the region is protected and attracts visitors looking for nature, hiking, wildlife and some of Taiwan's most beautiful mountain scenery.

The Famous Yellow Train

One of Taipingshan's most iconic attractions is the small yellow Bong Bong Train, a former logging railway that now carries visitors through the forest. The train slowly winds through the mountains, crossing bridges and passing towering trees that once supplied Taiwan's timber industry.

Although the ride is short, it feels incredibly charming and offers a glimpse into the region's logging history.

Misty Forests & Moss Everywhere

When we visited, Taipingshan was covered in mist. The clouds drifted through the forest, creating an almost magical atmosphere. Every tree trunk, rock and fallen branch seemed to be covered in vibrant green moss.

The region receives abundant rainfall throughout the year, creating the perfect conditions for lush vegetation. In some places, the scenery felt more like a temperate rainforest than what most people imagine when they think of Taiwan.

One of Taiwan's Most Beautiful Walks

  • Cueifong Lake Trail is often considered one of Taiwan's most beautiful forest walks.
  • The trail circles Taiwan's largest high-mountain lake.
  • Wooden boardwalks pass through ancient forests covered in moss and ferns.
  • Fog frequently rolls across the lake, creating dreamlike scenery.
  • The trail is accessible for most visitors while still feeling completely immersed in nature.

Hot Springs

The mountains around Taipingshan are also famous for their hot springs. The most popular is Jiuzhize Hot Spring, where visitors can relax in naturally heated mineral water surrounded by forested mountains.

A local specialty is cooking eggs and vegetables directly in the geothermal spring water, something many visitors try after a day of hiking.

Fun Facts

  • Taipingshan was once one of Taiwan's three largest logging regions.
  • The Bong Bong Train originally transported timber instead of tourists.
  • Cueifong Lake sits at an altitude of nearly 2,000 meters.
  • The area is frequently covered in mist due to its mountainous climate.
  • The forests contain ancient cypress trees that can be hundreds of years old.
  • Taipingshan is home to flying squirrels, monkeys and numerous bird species.
  • The region receives some of the highest rainfall levels in Taiwan.
  • Many visitors compare the moss-covered forests to scenes from Studio Ghibli films.
  • The cool mountain climate offers a refreshing escape from Taiwan's tropical heat.

Our Impression

Taipingshan felt completely different from anywhere else we visited in Taiwan. While much of the island is known for bustling cities, beaches and night markets, here we found silence, forests and clouds drifting between the trees.

The misty weather turned out to be a blessing. The forests looked magical, covered in moss and disappearing into the fog. Riding the little yellow train through the mountains and walking around Cueifong Lake felt like stepping into another world. Combined with the nearby hot springs, Taipingshan became one of the most peaceful and memorable places of our trip.
