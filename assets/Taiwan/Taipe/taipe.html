Taipei

Taipei is one of Asia's most underrated capitals. Surrounded by mountains, hot springs, rivers and hiking trails, the city combines world-class food, cutting-edge technology, rich history and a surprisingly relaxed atmosphere. It is the political, economic and cultural heart of Taiwan and home to more than 2.5 million people.

History

Long before Taipei existed, northern Taiwan was inhabited by indigenous peoples. Large-scale migration from China's Fujian province began during the 17th and 18th centuries, bringing the ancestors of many modern Taiwanese families.

Taiwan was ruled by Japan between 1895 and 1945, a period that heavily influenced Taipei's urban planning, railways, architecture and public infrastructure. After the Chinese Civil War in 1949, the Kuomintang (KMT) government relocated to Taiwan and Taipei became the capital of the Republic of China.

During the following decades Taiwan transformed from an authoritarian state into one of Asia's strongest democracies and one of the world's leading high-tech economies.

Night Market Culture

Night markets are one of Taipei's defining features. Every evening, entire streets transform into giant outdoor food courts where locals gather to eat, socialize and shop.

  • Shilin Night Market – The largest and most famous.
  • Raohe Night Market – One of the oldest and most atmospheric.
  • Ningxia Night Market – Known for traditional Taiwanese food.
  • Linjiang Night Market – Popular with locals.
  • Nanjichang Night Market – Less touristy and very authentic.

Food & Michelin Culture

Taipei is a paradise for food lovers. The city has one of the highest concentrations of Michelin-starred and Michelin-recommended restaurants in Asia. What makes Taipei unique is that incredible food exists at every price point—from luxury tasting menus to humble street food stalls.

  • Xiao Long Bao (Soup Dumplings)
  • Beef Noodle Soup
  • Scallion Pancakes
  • Bubble Tea
  • Lu Rou Fan (Braised Pork Rice)
  • Stinky Tofu
  • Oyster Omelette
  • Fried Chicken Cutlets

Language & Culture

  • Official Language: Mandarin Chinese
  • Taiwanese Hokkien is widely spoken.
  • Many older residents also speak some Japanese.
  • Taiwan is home to 16 officially recognized indigenous peoples.
  • Traditional Chinese characters are used instead of simplified characters.
  • English proficiency is increasing rapidly among younger generations.

Statistics & Daily Life

  • Population: ~2.5 million (7+ million in Greater Taipei).
  • Average summer temperatures often exceed 30°C.
  • Annual rainfall exceeds 2,000 mm.
  • Taipei has one of Asia's best public transportation systems.
  • Monthly city-center rent for a one-bedroom apartment can range from €700–€1,500+.
  • Taiwan is home to the world's largest semiconductor manufacturer.
  • Thousands of foreign professionals and digital nomads live in Taipei.

Fun Facts

  • Taipei 101 was the tallest building in the world from 2004 to 2010.
  • Taiwan invented modern bubble tea.
  • Garbage trucks play classical music to signal their arrival.
  • The city is surrounded by dormant volcanoes.
  • Taipei consistently ranks among the safest major cities in the world.
  • Taipei has one of the highest convenience-store densities in the world. In some neighborhoods, you can find multiple 7-Elevens and FamilyMarts within a few hundred meters of each other.
  • Many convenience stores do far more than sell snacks: locals use them to pay bills, print documents, buy train tickets, collect online orders, and even send packages.
  • Taiwan produces over 60% of the world's semiconductors.
  • The island sits on the Pacific Ring of Fire and experiences frequent earthquakes.
  • You can hike mountains, visit hot springs, relax on beaches and explore a modern city all within a single day.

Our Impression

Taipei quickly became one of our favorite cities in Asia. It feels modern, organized and incredibly easy to navigate, yet it never loses its local character. Every neighborhood seems to have its own personality, from bustling night markets to quiet temple courtyards hidden between skyscrapers.

What impressed us most was the balance. Few cities offer such a combination of amazing food, mountain hikes, efficient transport, safety and cultural richness. One moment you are eating at a Michelin-recommended restaurant, and an hour later you are standing on a mountain overlooking the entire city. Taipei felt like a place where we could easily imagine living for a longer time.
