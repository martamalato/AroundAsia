Hong Kong

Overview

Skyscrapers, mountains & one of Asia’s most layered identities

Hong Kong is one of the most fascinating places in Asia: a city of glass towers, neon streets, mountain trails, temples, ferries, finance, Cantonese food and complex political history.

What makes Hong Kong so unique is the contrast. It feels intensely urban, but around 70% of the territory is mountains, forests, reservoirs, country parks and undeveloped land. One moment you are surrounded by skyscrapers; the next you can be hiking above the sea.

Daily Life

Dim sum, ferries, vertical living & mountain escapes

Daily life in Hong Kong is fast, vertical and incredibly efficient. People move through dense streets, metro stations, food halls, markets and towers with a rhythm that feels both chaotic and organized.

Cantonese Food Culture

Hong Kong is a paradise for Cantonese food: dim sum, roast goose, wonton noodles, pineapple buns, egg tarts and strong milk tea. Even tiny local restaurants can serve food with world-class precision.

Mountain City

Despite the skyline, Hong Kong is full of nature. Hiking trails, beaches and islands are part of local life, making the city feel like a mix between financial capital and tropical mountain escape.

Vertical Living

Hong Kong grew upward because land is limited. Residential towers, office blocks, shopping malls and metro stations are stacked together in one of the world’s most vertical urban landscapes.

Star Ferry & Harbour Life

The Star Ferry has connected Hong Kong Island and Kowloon since the colonial era. Crossing Victoria Harbour is still one of the simplest and most beautiful ways to understand the city.

History

From Opium Wars to “One Country, Two Systems”

Hong Kong’s modern identity was shaped by war, trade, colonialism and its unusual return to China. Its legal and political status is one of the most important reasons the city feels different from mainland China.

1839 – 1842
First Opium War

Britain fought Qing China after disputes over the opium trade. China’s defeat led to the Treaty of Nanking, which ceded Hong Kong Island to Britain.

1860
Kowloon Added

After the Second Opium War, Britain gained control of the Kowloon Peninsula, expanding the colonial settlement beyond Hong Kong Island.

1898
The New Territories Lease

Britain leased the New Territories from China for 99 years. This lease became central to the eventual 1997 handover.

1941 – 1945
Japanese Occupation

During World War II, Hong Kong was occupied by Japan, causing hardship, population decline and economic disruption.

1997
Handover to China

Britain returned Hong Kong to China. Hong Kong became a Special Administrative Region under the principle of “One Country, Two Systems”.

1997 – 2047
The SAR Period

Hong Kong was promised a high degree of autonomy, with its own legal system, currency, immigration controls and civil liberties for 50 years after the handover.

Today
The Future Question

The big debate is what Hong Kong’s identity and autonomy will look like as 2047 approaches. In recent years, Beijing’s influence has increased, making the city’s future a major political question.

Urban Legends

Kowloon Walled City & Chungking Mansions

Some of Hong Kong’s most fascinating places are not just tourist spots, but urban legends. They tell the story of density, migration, survival, crime, cinema and globalization.

Kowloon Walled City

Once a legal grey zone, Kowloon Walled City became one of the most densely populated places on Earth. By the 1980s, tens of thousands lived in a chaotic maze of dark corridors, rooftop extensions, factories, dentists, gambling rooms and unregulated buildings.

Cyberpunk Inspiration

Its dark, layered, almost impossible architecture inspired films, anime, video games and cyberpunk aesthetics. It was demolished in the 1990s and replaced by a public park, but its image still shapes how people imagine futuristic urban chaos.

Chungking Mansions

We stayed at Chungking Mansions in Tsim Sha Tsui, one of Hong Kong’s most famous buildings. It is packed with guesthouses, curry restaurants, money changers, traders, backpackers and immigrants from all over the world.

From Notorious to Iconic

In the 1970s and 1980s, Chungking Mansions had a reputation for crime, gangs, smuggling and counterfeit goods. Today it is much safer, but still feels intense, multicultural and cinematic. It also inspired Wong Kar-wai’s iconic film Chungking Express.

Food

Pineapple buns, roast goose & milk tea

Hong Kong food is one of the best ways to understand the city: Cantonese traditions, British colonial habits, street snacks, luxury restaurants and tiny family-run places all exist side by side.

  • Pineapple Bun: Despite the name, it contains no pineapple. The name comes from the golden, crunchy crust.
  • Dim Sum: Small dishes served with tea, one of the most important Cantonese food traditions.
  • Roast Goose: Crispy skin, rich meat and one of Hong Kong’s most famous dishes.
  • Egg Tarts: A sweet pastry shaped by British and Portuguese influences.
  • Milk Tea: Strong black tea with evaporated milk, smooth, bold and very local.

Yat Lok Roast Goose

One of Hong Kong’s most famous roast goose spots, known for its Michelin recognition and its intense, crispy, juicy Cantonese roast style.

Food as Identity

In Hong Kong, food is not just eating. It is family, speed, memory, status, comfort and daily routine, from luxury dim sum halls to simple noodle shops.

Culture

Horse racing, cinema & Cantonese identity

Hong Kong culture is shaped by Cantonese traditions, British colonial influence, finance, migration, cinema and its role as a bridge between China and the wider world.

Happy Valley Horse Racing

Horse racing was introduced under British rule and remains one of Hong Kong’s most iconic social events. Wednesday nights at Happy Valley feel like a mix of sport, outdoor party and city ritual, surrounded by skyscrapers.

Hong Kong Cinema

Hong Kong shaped global cinema through action films, martial arts, crime dramas and directors like Wong Kar-wai. The city’s density, neon and mood made it one of the most cinematic places in Asia.

Fun Facts

Things that make Hong Kong unforgettable

  • Hong Kong has its own currency, legal system and immigration rules.
  • Nearly 70% of the territory is mountains, forests and protected nature.
  • The city is famous for having one of the world’s most dramatic skylines.
  • Kowloon Walled City was once among the densest places ever built.
  • The Star Ferry has crossed Victoria Harbour since the 19th century.
  • Hong Kong has more than 260 islands.
  • Cantonese is central to local identity, even though Mandarin and English are also important.
  • Many apartments are extremely small because land is limited and housing prices are high.
